? Ha, you've put your finger on something genuinely real and a bit paradoxical.
  ?? Let me be honest about it:

  You're right about the asymmetry

  For C++ history, there are thousands of consistent, cross-checkable sources —
  books, interviews with Stroustrup, Bell Labs records. The internet "remembers"
  it, and I absorbed that consensus. Solid ground.

  But questions about me specifically are trickier for a few reasons:

  1. I wasn't there for my own making. I don't have memories of my training, no
  diary of "and then they adjusted my weights." I can't introspect on my own
  internals the way you might think. When you ask "why did you say X?", I can
  give a plausible-sounding reason, but I genuinely can't be sure it's the
  actual mechanism — it might be a story I'm reconstructing after the fact.
  (Humans do this too, by the way — confabulation is well-studied in
  psychology.)
  2. The training data is mostly older than me. Text about "AI" on the internet
  is full of sci-fi, older systems, hype, and speculation — not accurate
  documentation of this specific model. So if I lean on "what the internet says
  about AI," I risk describing a fictional robot, not myself.
  3. The reliable stuff about me comes from Anthropic, not the open web — model
  cards, system instructions, docs. That's a narrower, more curated source than
  the vast consensus that backs up "C came after B."

  The genuinely weird part

  There's a real philosophical puzzle here: I can fluently discuss the concept
  of large language models in general, but I have no privileged "inside view" of
  being one. It's a bit like asking someone to describe their own brain's
  neuron firings — you can read the neuroscience, but you can't feel the
  synapses.

  So the honest answer is: on the outside world, I'm a well-read generalist; on
  myself, I'm more like someone working from secondhand documentation and a lot
  of uncertainty. And I think the right move is to just say so rather than
  confidently make things up. ??

  It's one of the more interesting things about talking to an AI, isn't it?
